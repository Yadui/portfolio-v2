# Content Strategy

## Purpose

The portfolio should communicate capability quickly to recruiters, hiring managers, and potential clients. Content needs to convert attention into trust, then into action.

## Primary Audiences

- Recruiters screening for role fit and proof of execution
- Hiring managers evaluating depth, ownership, and communication
- Clients looking for service clarity and delivery confidence
- Technical peers discovering blog posts or project writeups

## Messaging Pillars

- Build quality: polished interfaces, production-ready implementation
- Creative engineering: motion, interaction, and distinct frontend identity
- Full-stack credibility: content, APIs, auth, and data-backed features
- Outcomes: measurable impact, not just tool lists

## Page Goals

| Page | Primary goal | Required content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Create a memorable first impression and route users deeper | Role statement, strongest proof point, selected work, CTA |
| Work | Show depth and problem-solving ability | Case studies with challenge, process, result |
| Services | Explain what can be hired for | Service offers, process, timeline, CTA |
| Resume | Support quick qualification review | Summary, download option, highlights |
| Blog | Build authority and discoverability | Articles tied to engineering, design, or process |
| Contact | Convert interest into inquiry | Contact paths, expected response, low-friction form |

## Home Page Narrative Order

1. Who you are.
2. What kind of problems you solve.
3. Proof through featured work.
4. Credibility through experience and skills.
5. Contact path.

## Work Case Study Template

Each featured project or role should cover:

- Context: client, product, team, or problem space
- Challenge: what was difficult or constrained
- Contribution: what you specifically owned
- Stack: technologies used
- Outcome: metrics, quality improvements, or user impact
- Reflection: what makes the project worth showing

## Writing Rules

- Start with value, not biography.
- Prefer concrete claims over adjectives.
- Convert tool lists into capability statements.
- Keep paragraphs short enough for skim reading.
- Use numbers wherever a result can be measured.

## CTA Strategy

Use one primary CTA family across the site:

- Recruiter CTA: view work, download resume, contact
- Client CTA: discuss a project, review services, contact

Secondary CTAs can support blog exploration or deep links into work details, but they should not compete visually with the primary path.

## Asset Checklist

- Updated resume PDF
- Optimized project thumbnails
- Work detail screenshots or supporting visuals
- Open Graph image system
- Social profile links
- Company logos or trust marks where allowed

## Editorial Backlog Ideas

- How the skills visualization was designed and tuned
- Lessons from building motion-heavy Next.js interfaces
- Writing maintainable UI systems in mixed JS and TS codebases
- Building portfolio work pages that recruiters can skim in under two minutes
- Notes on auth, blog tooling, or content workflows in a personal site

## Content Governance

- Review homepage copy quarterly.
- Refresh featured work whenever a stronger case study is available.
- Keep services and resume aligned with the roles you actively want.
- Archive outdated blog or portfolio content instead of letting it drift.
